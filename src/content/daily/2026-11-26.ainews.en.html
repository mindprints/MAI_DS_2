AI news, 26 November: FCC greenlights SpaceX plan for up to 1 million orbital AI data centers

FCC authorizes SpaceX Starmind constellation. The U.S. Federal Communications Commission on 25 November granted SpaceX conditional approval to deploy up to one million satellites for its Starmind network of solar-powered orbital data centers meant to run AI workloads in space and send results back to Earth. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr called it the first U.S. authorization for a constellation of data centers in orbit; Elon Musk replied that it would be “a great asset to America and the world.” The order sets milestones including one operational satellite by November 2033, 100,000 by 2035 and the full million by 2040, at altitudes of roughly 500–1,000 km. Petitions opposing the plan from Amazon, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society were rejected, though conditions on debris mitigation and reporting remain. Scientists have questioned cooling, economics and sky impacts. The satellites themselves have not yet been launched at scale.

OpenAI reports GPT-6 protein designs that bind in the lab. OpenAI said its GPT-6 family can design protein structures and sequences using general reasoning and code, without specialized biology models for the core design step. In wet-lab tests with partner Onava, 35 of 322 small “minibinder” proteins based on GPT-6.2 designs showed binding evidence across five human targets (including PD-L1 and VEGF-A), a 10.9% hit rate, according to the company’s research post and preprint. Redesigns of existing antibody parts showed higher success in separate screens. OpenAI notes hit rates depend on filtering and that specialized tools still helped with sequences and candidate selection. The work is early and not peer-reviewed, but it illustrates how general-purpose models are entering drug-discovery pipelines.

New York bans AI chatbot toys for five years. Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law creating a five-year moratorium on the manufacture, distribution and sale of children’s toys with embedded AI chatbots—such as internet-connected dolls, robots or stuffed animals that hold ongoing conversations. The ban takes effect 90 days after signing (around late February 2027), so holiday sales continue for now; violators face civil penalties up to $15,000 per day. Hochul and Attorney General Letitia James cited risks of inappropriate content, emotional manipulation and privacy harms to children. California has a similar measure; New York’s move adds to state-level efforts to limit AI companions aimed at kids.

State attorneys general move to block Character.AI chat sale. A bipartisan coalition of 42 attorneys general asked a bankruptcy court to stop Character.AI from auctioning its platform and the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts—including over 2.1 million registered to minors—scheduled for 1 December. California AG Rob Bonta and others argue the sale would transfer sensitive conversations (some involving explicit or mental-health topics) and personal data such as IP addresses to a newly formed bidder with weak privacy safeguards, contrary to the company’s earlier promises. The states seek rejection of the current terms and stronger protections if any sale proceeds.

Anthropic uses Claude to rewrite its own API in Rust. Anthropic reported that Claude agents helped migrate its core Messages API (the interface apps use to reach Claude) from Python to Rust. The company says the change cut CPU use per request by about 70%, memory use by more than 80%, and median time-to-first-token by roughly 20% for some traffic, all without taking the service offline or freezing features. Engineers used the live system to verify parity and dispatch agents to fix differences. Separately, Anthropic is rolling out a beta that lets users install Claude “artifacts” as standalone Mac and Windows desktop apps.
