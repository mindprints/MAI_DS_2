AI news, 21 November: Anthropic mathematician claims Claude helped prove the Riemann hypothesis

Anthropic researcher claims AI-assisted Riemann proof. Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, stated on Friday that he had discovered a proof of the Riemann hypothesis — one of mathematics’ most famous unsolved problems, posed in 1859 and carrying a $1 million Clay Millennium Prize — while working with the company’s Claude model. According to New Scientist, the Financial Times and TechCrunch, he released a large formalization in the Lean proof-checking language rather than a human-readable paper, saying a readable version would exceed 250,000 pages. He credited Claude with key late-stage advances. The claim remains unverified; experts note that Lean code must still be carefully checked by specialists, and the Clay Institute requires years of scrutiny before any prize. A separate human-only proof was also reported around the same time.

Trump threatens tax on overseas AI data centres. U.S. President Donald Trump said Friday that American companies building data centres abroad would face a “tremendous tax,” arguing the move would keep AI infrastructure in the United States and prevent China from benefiting. Reuters and other outlets reported he made the comments at a White House briefing after an Asia trip, without detailing the rate, legal mechanism or timeline. The threat comes amid domestic pushback against data-centre expansion over energy use and local impacts, and could affect major cloud providers already investing overseas.

Google adds computer-use agent to Gemini on Mac. Google began rolling out a feature that lets Gemini control Mac apps, files and websites in a background window while the user continues working. According to the company blog and TechCrunch, users describe multi-step tasks in plain language; the agent clicks, types and navigates under live preview, with permissions, pause controls and approval for sensitive actions. It is initially available in English to U.S. Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra subscribers on Apple Silicon Macs. Google cautions that mistakes, including unintended file changes, remain possible.

Thinking Machines releases laptop-friendly open model. Thinking Machines Lab launched Inkling 2 Mini, a 26.4-billion-parameter open-weights mixture-of-experts model designed to run on consumer laptops (roughly 20 GB memory footprint). The lab said the “looped” architecture reuses middle layers for stronger reasoning per token and reported leading scores among open models under 100 billion parameters on several agentic coding benchmarks. Weights are available on Hugging Face under a permissive license, with support for local tools such as Ollama.

AI stocks slide as Anthropic shares pull back. A broader sell-off in artificial-intelligence-related shares dragged major U.S. indices lower on Friday, with the Nasdaq erasing its 2026 gains in one report. Anthropic shares, which had risen sharply on their market debut the previous day after the record IPO, fell several percent, according to Bloomberg and other market coverage — a reminder that high valuations face ongoing investor scrutiny over costs and returns.
