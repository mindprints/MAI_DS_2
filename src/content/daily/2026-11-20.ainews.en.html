AI news, 20 November: DeepSeek raises $15 billion ahead of Shanghai IPO

DeepSeek closes massive funding round. Chinese AI lab DeepSeek has completed a second external funding round of roughly 100 billion yuan (about $15 billion), according to Caixin Global, and is preparing an initial public offering on Shanghai’s STAR Market. Business registration updates show new investors taking a stake of around 17 percent. The raise, finalized in recent days, underscores how Chinese developers are securing large-scale capital to compete globally even as U.S. export limits restrict access to advanced chips. It comes the same week as major U.S. AI listings and highlights the intensifying international race for computing power and talent.

Google rolls out Gemini 4 Neon. Google DeepMind has launched Gemini 4 Neon, a new model aimed at coding, analysis, and complex knowledge work. It features a one-million-token context window, four adjustable “thinking” levels, and pricing of $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens. Google says Neon ranks near the top of independent benchmarks such as the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index while delivering tasks at notably lower cost and higher speed than its heavier sibling model Carbon. The model is available via the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, business tools, and paid Gemini app subscriptions.

Banks shift AI-agent liability to customers. Citibank, PNC, and Citizens have updated their terms so that customers who authorize third-party AI agents to access their accounts bear responsibility for resulting actions and losses—even if the agent is hacked or leaks credentials—according to TechCrunch and bank statements. Citi carves out an exception for its own agents. JPMorgan and Bank of America made similar moves earlier. The changes reflect growing caution as AI tools begin handling real financial tasks, placing the risk firmly on users rather than the banks.

Nvidia halts licensed AI-chip shipments to China. Interim CEO Colette Kress said on the company’s earnings call that Nvidia is no longer shipping licensed Hopper-generation data-center products, including H200 chips, to China, and its guidance assumes zero such revenue. The firm sold $2.4 billion of those products in the prior quarter. The decision follows U.S. approvals that had allowed limited sales and comes amid broader export-rule changes and geopolitical tensions, according to Reuters and company remarks. Nvidia also reported record quarterly revenue of $115.8 billion, driven by data-center demand.

Update: Anthropic’s first trading day. Shares of Anthropic, which priced its record IPO at $83 and raised about $81 billion, opened at $111.50 under ticker ANT and closed at $97.17 on 19 November, according to market reports. CEO Dario Amodei used the Nasdaq debut to reaffirm the company’s commitment to pacing AI development so systems remain under human control. The listing remains the largest U.S. IPO on record but is covered here only as a follow-up to yesterday’s lead.
