AI news, 23 November: OpenAI calls for shared limits on frontier AI development

OpenAI proposes industry-wide AI development limits. OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published internal model measurements on 22 November and called on leading AI labs to agree on common limits tied to a shared progress metric called perplexity (how well a model predicts the next piece of text). According to Reuters and TechCrunch, the proposal—backed by CEO Sam Altman—would have labs pause crossing certain thresholds until better tools exist to monitor AI behaviour. Pachocki linked falling perplexity scores to new capabilities in areas such as mathematics and cybersecurity, and warned that further advances could arrive as soon as 2027. The post marks a rare public sharing of OpenAI’s internal tracking numbers and frames the idea as a voluntary industry standard rather than regulation.

Update: Lean community certifies Claude-assisted Riemann proof. The Lean and Mathlib communities, endorsed by the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative, issued a joint statement confirming that both the statement and the formal proof of the Riemann hypothesis released by Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge are correct. Alpöge had said he developed the roughly 17-million-line Lean library while working with Claude. Independent checkers and community verification support the result; broader mathematical peer review and any official prize recognition remain separate steps. This turns the earlier claim into a machine-checked formal result.

OpenRouter drops providers after prompt-leak findings. OpenRouter removed three inference providers and stripped 125 endpoints of zero-data-retention status after security firm Dragos reported that attackers had accessed customer prompts—including some marked private—at multiple hosting services over months. Dragos seeded about two million test prompts with hidden markers; OpenRouter said its own systems were not breached and has notified potentially affected customers. Providers including Together AI have disclosed related incidents. The episode highlights ongoing risks when routing sensitive queries through third-party AI hosts.

Sarvam releases upgraded open-weight 105B model. Indian lab Sarvam AI launched Sarvam-105B-1711, a new checkpoint of its mixture-of-experts model that the company says scores substantially higher on agent, instruction-following and Indian-language benchmarks. A compressed version is designed to run on a single high-end workstation GPU, and weights are available under an open licence. Sarvam also cut its reported blended serving price. The release targets high-volume enterprise uses such as customer support and voice agents.

South Korea education minister resigns amid AI cheating fallout. Education Minister Choi Kyo-jin resigned after last-minute anti-cheating rules for the national college entrance exam (Suneung) caused widespread confusion, following an earlier tax-exam scandal in which the top scorer admitted using a hidden camera and AI. President Lee Jae Myung accepted the resignation. Authorities reported dozens of device-related incidents during the exam taken by nearly half a million students. The episode underscores growing pressure on education systems to address AI-assisted cheating.
