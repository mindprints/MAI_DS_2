AI news, 27 November: US chip curbs and Nvidia pause slow Chinese AI labs

US chip rules and distillation limits delay Chinese flagships. According to a Reuters exclusive, Chinese AI developers Z.AI and Moonshot AI are facing significant delays on next-generation models. Z.AI’s planned GLM-5.5, designed with roughly 3 trillion parameters, has missed its November target and may slip to the first quarter of 2027 or later, partly due to stronger anti-distillation safeguards by OpenAI and Anthropic. Moonshot AI expects its usual six-month release cycle could stretch to a year or longer if overseas computing access is cut off, potentially pushing Kimi K4 into mid-2027. The pressure stems from a US Commerce Department rule effective 19 November that requires licenses for large-scale overseas use of advanced American chips, closing a key low-cost route Chinese labs had used.

Nvidia halts AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries. Nvidia has told manufacturing partners including Dell and Supermicro to pause shipments of servers with its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times and Wall Street Journal reported. The step follows the new US export controls aimed at stopping Chinese firms from remotely accessing high-end Nvidia processors through regional data centers—an approach estimated by some analysts to have supplied around a quarter of China’s AI compute. It is unclear whether the halt is temporary while companies work through licensing and compliance.

Australia enacts digital duty of care that covers AI chatbots. Australia’s Parliament has passed the Online Safety Amendment (Digital Duty of Care) Bill 2026. It requires online services—including AI chatbots, social media, games and apps—to take reasonable steps to prevent serious harms and protect children from addictive features and companions that simulate emotional intimacy. Penalties can reach 5% of global revenue or A$109.2 million (whichever is greater), and individuals gain a right to sue for breaches. The rules take effect 12 months after royal assent, according to the government.

Japan drafts enforceable safety duties for frontier AI providers. Japan is preparing amendments to its AI Promotion Act that would impose safety obligations, accident reporting and possible on-site inspections or corrective orders on high-performance AI developers, including foreign firms such as OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Alibaba and DeepSeek, Nikkei and Yomiuri reported. The draft, aimed at the 2027 ordinary Diet session, would move beyond purely voluntary guidance and could include penalties for ignoring government orders. Officials cite rising AI-enabled cyberattacks as a driver.

Japan’s JERA talks power-plant data centers with Anthropic and Meta. Japan’s largest electricity producer JERA is in discussions with Anthropic, Meta and other US tech companies including Amazon, Microsoft and Google about building AI data centers next to its Tokyo-area power plants, Nikkei reported. The “behind-the-meter” model would let facilities draw power directly from generation sites, easing grid bottlenecks. JERA already has a memorandum of understanding with Dell and UK developer RHAELM for a potential multi-billion-dollar hyperscale project starting in Chiba.
