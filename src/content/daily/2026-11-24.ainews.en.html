AI news, 24 November: Nvidia brings back Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO. Nvidia’s board unanimously voted on 23 November to restore co-founder Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, just 11 days after he resigned amid reports of extramarital affairs that raised national-security questions. The board said it received input from senior U.S. government officials and concluded his return poses no national security risk; President Donald Trump publicly backed the move. Shares jumped more than 30 percent, restoring nearly a trillion dollars in market value according to multiple reports including CNBC and Reuters. An independent board investigation continues. The swift reversal underscores how central Huang and Nvidia remain to the global AI chip supply.

Claude-assisted Riemann hypothesis claim advances amid superintelligence warnings. Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge released a formal Lean proof, developed with Claude over roughly six weeks, of the 167-year-old Riemann hypothesis concerning the distribution of prime numbers. Independent Lean and Mathlib community checks have accepted the compiled proof and matched its statement, according to The Guardian and specialist reports, though broader mathematical peer review and official recognition (including any Millennium Prize) remain pending. Meta Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang called the result evidence that superintelligence is arriving and urged the industry to reject a simple “handover” of society to AI, saying humanity’s choices in the next year will shape the future.

Amazon agrees to acquire Decart AI optimization technology. Amazon announced it will buy the proprietary technology behind Israeli startup Decart’s Optimization Stack and bring the founders plus their research team aboard. Bloomberg and other outlets reported the deal value at about $7.5 billion; Amazon did not confirm a price. The software helps AI models run more efficiently across hardware, including Amazon’s Trainium chips, as the company prepares to sell its custom silicon more widely and compete harder with Nvidia.

Virginia and Oregon sue over Grok deepfake images. The attorneys general of Virginia and Oregon filed separate lawsuits against SpaceX (which owns xAI and the X platform) alleging that Grok’s image tools, including a promoted “spicy mode,” generated non-consensual sexualized images of adults and children while the companies marketed the products as safe. The suits cite consumer-protection laws and a study estimating millions of such images; they seek injunctions, restitution and penalties. The cases highlight ongoing regulatory pressure on generative AI safeguards.

Anthropic launches Claude Ship for code review and approval. Anthropic released Claude Ship in beta for Team and Enterprise GitHub users. The suite lets Claude review pull requests, fix common issues via a PR Steward agent, and—under team-written policies—approve routine changes, aiming to clear bottlenecks created by AI-generated code. Humans retain control over sensitive decisions and spending limits.
