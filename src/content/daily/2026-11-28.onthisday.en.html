1660: The Royal Society is founded

On 28 November 1660, after a lecture by Christopher Wren at Gresham College in London, twelve men agreed to form a “College for the Promoting of Physico-Mathematical Experimental Learning.” That meeting is the conventional founding moment of the Royal Society of London, still one of the world’s oldest scientific academies.

The group—including Robert Boyle, John Wilkins and William Petty—wanted knowledge advanced by experiment, measurement and open debate rather than by authority. Their later motto, Nullius in verba (“take nobody’s word for it”), captured the shift. Within a few years the Society received a royal charter and launched Philosophical Transactions, the first enduring scientific journal.

The link to artificial intelligence is indirect yet real. The Royal Society helped institutionalise the mathematical and mechanical culture on which computing rests. Isaac Newton, a later Fellow, supplied calculus and physical law. In the nineteenth century Fellow Charles Babbage designed the Analytical Engine; Ada Lovelace’s notes on that machine, circulating in the same scientific milieu, first described general-purpose computation. In the twentieth century Alan Turing (FRS 1951) and other Fellows clarified computability, while the broader empirical ethos shaped information theory and cybernetics.

The Society’s demand for precise language and reproducible results also fed the rise of formal logic. The clarity prized in its pages became part of the intellectual climate in which Frege, Russell and their successors built the symbolic systems that underpin programming languages and automated reasoning.

Modern AI still draws on that cumulative tradition: algorithms refined under peer scrutiny, hardware grounded in physical principles first systematically explored in the Society’s orbit, and an insistence on testing ideas against evidence. When today’s models parse language or robots act in the world, they inherit, however quietly, the experimental turn begun on that November day in 1660.
