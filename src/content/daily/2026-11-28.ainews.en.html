AI news, 28 November: OpenAI countersues Apple over trade secrets and suppliers

OpenAI countersues Apple in hardware secrets battle. OpenAI filed counterclaims in California federal court on 27 November, accusing Apple of trade-secret theft and pressuring shared hardware suppliers to limit work on OpenAI devices, according to the Financial Times, Reuters and Bloomberg. The move escalates Apple’s July lawsuit alleging that two former Apple employees took confidential design information to OpenAI’s hardware efforts (including work linked to Jony Ive’s io Products). OpenAI claims a returning employee transferred over 100 confidential files about its unreleased products and that Apple used aggressive employment practices and supplier pressure to hinder rivals. Apple called the counterclaims a distraction and said OpenAI had already been ordered to stop using its information. The dispute highlights intensifying competition as AI labs move into consumer devices.

SAP weighs at least 10,000 job cuts in AI restructuring. German software giant SAP is preparing to cut at least 10,000 positions—about 9% of its roughly 110,000-strong workforce—as it pivots to an AI-first model, Bloomberg reported citing people familiar with the matter. Product, engineering and sales roles are under particular focus; final numbers could change after labour talks. SAP said it continuously reviews its organisation for the AI era but declined to comment on specific plans. Similar large cuts have occurred at other software firms adapting to AI automation of coding, support and sales work.

Update: Nvidia pauses AI server shipments to four Southeast Asian countries. Nvidia has instructed partners including Dell and Supermicro to halt shipments of servers containing its chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported. The pause follows new US Commerce rules requiring licences for advanced chips destined for data centres in those countries (and Brazil), aimed at closing loopholes used by Chinese firms to rent compute abroad. It is unclear how long the halt will last while compliance is sorted. This extends recent US export controls that have already slowed Chinese AI labs’ access to high-end hardware.

Anthropic shares rebound above IPO price. Shares in Anthropic closed nearly 11% higher at $91.88 on 27 November, reclaiming ground above the $83 IPO price set on 19 November, Bloomberg and other market reports said. The move came amid a broader short-session rally in AI-related stocks after a volatile first week that had seen the stock dip below the offer price. Analysts including Wedbush, Canaccord and Oppenheimer initiated coverage with targets ranging from $120 to $150. The rebound offers a snapshot of still-strong investor interest despite broader jitters over AI spending and valuations.

Los Angeles schools block Google AI search features. Los Angeles Unified School District, the second-largest in the US, has temporarily disabled Google’s AI Overviews (auto-generated search summaries) on student devices using third-party filters, the Los Angeles Times and Associated Press reported. The district cited age-appropriate and safety concerns under its existing generative-AI moratorium. Google has indicated AI is now integral to search and has resisted offering a clean non-AI version for schools, prompting some districts to explore alternatives or workarounds. The standoff reflects wider debate over AI use by children in classrooms.
