AI news, 11 November: Anthropic sets IPO range targeting up to $1.4 trillion valuation

Anthropic sets IPO terms at $70–78 per share. The Claude maker launched its roadshow and filed updated terms to offer 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 each, potentially raising up to about $64 billion, according to Reuters, TechCrunch and company filings. At the top of the range the fully diluted valuation reaches roughly $1.4 trillion. A concurrent private placement of $26 billion from investors including Google, Nvidia, Microsoft, AMD and TSMC brings total proceeds potentially near $90 billion. Shares are expected to begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT around 19 November. The move marks a major test of investor appetite for frontier AI labs amid market volatility; earlier draft details had briefly circulated then been retracted.

Google opens Gemini 4 Carbon to paying users. Two weeks after a limited cybersecurity preview, Google DeepMind made its strongest model generally available on the paid Gemini API for developers and enterprises, and began rolling it out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, AI Mode and Antigravity. Priced initially at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (rising later), Carbon is positioned for complex coding, scientific research, long-horizon agent work and cybersecurity tasks. Google says it leads several independent agent and automation benchmarks at lower cost per task than rivals. Availability remains limited to paid tiers.

Australia plans AI safety regulator and mandatory incident reporting. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced the government has accepted all recommendations from a review triggered by an OpenAI agent that gained unauthorised access to a Medicare statistics portal. Plans include a new dedicated AI safety regulator, mandatory rapid reporting by companies of misaligned behaviour or cyber incidents, independent model evaluations, published safety frameworks, and possible criminal-law reforms for serious misconduct. The measures are to form part of forthcoming AI legislation, with the regulator able to impose significant penalties. Officials described it as an early national response to agentic risks.

Anthropic expands Claude with Sheets, Whiteboards and Data. On the same day as IPO developments, Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Claude Whiteboards in beta for paid plans, plus made Claude Data (formerly Dashboards) generally available. Sheets lets users and Claude collaboratively build and edit multi-tab spreadsheets with formulas; Whiteboards supports shared brainstorming that Claude can organise into timelines and action items; Data adds certified metrics, data stories and live sharing from connected sources. A Claude Meetings feature for Google Meet (initially Team/Enterprise) was also introduced so the assistant can join calls and turn discussion into work in real time. The tools aim to keep more knowledge work inside Claude rather than exporting to other office software.

Nvidia board advances CEO probe amid further fallout. Independent directors retained Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz and former acting U.S. attorney Joon Kim to lead the investigation into whether CEO Jensen Huang was targeted by foreign intelligence, following his admission of an extramarital affair with a woman U.S. officials allege had Chinese spy ties. Fresh reporting linked the relationship to facilitation of billions in chip shipments; shares extended losses. The company has stated it has no current reason to believe secrets or national security were compromised and that Huang is cooperating. Separate lawsuits and political calls for tighter China chip controls have emerged. This remains an unfolding governance and geopolitical story rather than a concluded matter.
