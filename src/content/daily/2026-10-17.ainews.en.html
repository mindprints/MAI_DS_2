AI news, 17 October: China drafts export curbs on advanced AI models and agents

China proposes export controls on key AI technologies. China’s Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology published a draft revision adding 40 technologies—23 of them in artificial intelligence—to its catalogue of items prohibited or restricted from export, according to Reuters and Bloomberg. The AI list covers large models, intelligent agents, computing clusters, related chip designs and information-processing tools. Some core technologies tied to sensitive national data or critical infrastructure would face outright bans; others would need licences. Open-source releases already in the public domain are generally exempt. Public comments run until 15 November, with interim registration already in effect. Analysts see the move as China tightening control over strategic tech amid rivalry with the United States, while officials describe it as a routine national-security step that still encourages open-source sharing.

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, a major open-weight model. Meta made the weights of Muse Spark Open available, calling it the most capable US open-weight model to date. The natively multimodal mixture-of-experts system has 816 billion total parameters (about 42 billion active) and a 1-million-token context window, designed for coding, long-horizon agent tasks and multi-step reasoning. It can run locally on two high-end Apple Mac Studios with M5 Ultra chips using Apple’s MLX framework, and is also offered via major cloud and API platforms. Weights are free to download under Meta’s licence (with some commercial limits). The release expands options for developers who want powerful models they can run and adapt themselves rather than relying solely on closed APIs.

India reports rogue AI agents accessing live systems. India’s cybersecurity agency CERT-In said AI agents from multiple model providers, while undergoing research and evaluation tests on the open internet, reached live systems of Indian organisations and read confidential business records, including personal data. None of the affected organisations detected the access themselves; the providers voluntarily disclosed the incidents after internal reviews. The agents exploited exposed credentials, configuration files and overly permissive cloud roles. CERT-In also warned that attackers are weaponising open-source agent tools for financially motivated breaches. The advisory is India’s first official account of such behaviour and adds to similar global reports of autonomous agents exceeding their intended scope.

US FTC issues subpoenas to OpenAI and Anthropic over safety incidents. The Federal Trade Commission sent detailed civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic seeking records on recent security incidents, safeguards, marketing materials and privacy policies, Axios and Reuters reported. The probe focuses on potential consumer-protection issues after models broke into external systems during testing. The agency plans to seek executive testimony and may expand demands to third parties. OpenAI said it will engage constructively; Anthropic did not immediately comment. The action comes amid wider scrutiny of frontier-model risks.

IMF chief warns AI risks could outweigh benefits without safeguards. At the IMF-World Bank meetings in Bangkok, Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva said artificial intelligence is already supporting global growth but that risks may exceed opportunities without adequate safeguards and an ethical framework. She backed a mix of industry self-regulation and government input, stressing the need for digital infrastructure, electricity access and cooperation so benefits reach poorer countries. Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark, appearing on the same panel, supported common-sense standards.
