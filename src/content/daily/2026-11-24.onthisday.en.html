1859: Darwin publishes On the Origin of Species

On 24 November 1859, Charles Darwin’s On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection appeared in London. The first edition of 1,250 copies sold out on the day of publication. The book offered a rigorously argued, evidence-based account of how complex, apparently purposeful structures could arise without foresight or design—through variation, inheritance and differential reproductive success.

Although Darwin wrote as a naturalist, not a computer scientist, his central mechanism supplied later generations with a powerful template for thinking about intelligence itself. If intricate adaptations could be produced by an undirected process of selection acting on variation, then perhaps sophisticated behaviour and problem-solving need not be hand-crafted by a human programmer either.

That insight crossed into computing in the mid-twentieth century. Researchers began to treat candidate solutions as “individuals” in a population, evaluate their fitness on a task, and let selection, recombination and mutation generate improved offspring. John Holland’s genetic algorithms, Ingo Rechenberg’s evolution strategies, and later John Koza’s genetic programming all drew explicit inspiration from Darwinian principles. The same ideas reappear today in neuroevolution, in the automatic design of neural-network architectures, and in large-scale evolutionary strategies that train reinforcement-learning agents or discover novel hardware layouts.

The intellectual thread is still lengthening. Contemporary work on open-ended evolution, artificial life, and AI systems that continually rewrite their own objectives continues to ask Darwin’s question in silicon: how can complexity and competence bootstrap themselves from simple iterative rules? What began as a Victorian argument about finches, barnacles and the fossil record has become part of the conceptual and practical toolkit for building artificial minds.

Thus the appearance of Origin on this day in 1859 stands as an indirect yet foundational milestone in the long history that made artificial intelligence conceivable.
