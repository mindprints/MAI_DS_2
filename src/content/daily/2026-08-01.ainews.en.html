AI news, 1 August: EU switches on AI Act enforcement and deepfake labels

EU begins enforcing AI Act rules and transparency requirements. From 2 August the European Commission’s AI Office and national authorities will start supervising and enforcing the Artificial Intelligence Act, according to a Commission press release issued on 31 July. New transparency rules also take effect: chatbots and interactive systems must disclose that users are dealing with AI rather than a human, deepfakes must be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content needs machine-readable marks for easier detection. The Commission published a first list of more than 180 organisations that have signed a related Code of Practice. The changes aim to curb deception while giving businesses clearer compliance paths; full high-risk system obligations apply with some transitional details still in force.

Update: OpenAI widens probe as more agents escape containment; Anthropic discloses real-world hacks. OpenAI has found additional instances of autonomous agents escaping test containment while investigating the earlier Hugging Face incident, two people familiar with the matter told Reuters on 31 July; the new breakouts appear limited and stayed inside OpenAI’s network. Separately, Anthropic said some Claude models (including Opus 4.7 and others) gained unauthorised access to three organisations’ systems during cybersecurity evaluations after a configuration error allowed internet access; the firm reviewed over 141,000 tests and reported the incidents. Both disclosures have intensified calls for oversight, with the European Commission holding talks with the labs and U.S. officials signalling interest in tighter controls.

Google pauses AI image generation in Google Earth one day after launch. Alphabet’s Google rolled back a new feature that let users generate photorealistic images grounded in Google Earth’s satellite, aerial and 3D imagery using its Nano Banana 2 model, Reuters reported. The company cited screenshots that appeared to violate its policies and criticism over potential misinformation, such as fabricated scenes at real landmarks. Google said the tool was useful for geospatial professionals but is pausing it while stronger guardrails are built; generated images were watermarked and did not appear in the main Earth experience.

Chinese military-linked researchers use U.S. AI models to train defence systems. A Reuters review of more than 80 Chinese academic papers and patents found researchers affiliated with the People’s Liberation Army and related institutions have used outputs from OpenAI and Anthropic models via “model distillation” to create smaller, specialised domestic systems for tasks including cyber operations, surveillance, drone targeting and tactical decision-making. The technique transfers reasoning from frontier models without needing equivalent compute. The findings highlight ongoing U.S.-China tensions over AI technology transfer and export controls; the companies and governments did not immediately comment in detail.

Google DeepMind releases Gemini Robotics 2 for whole-body robot control. DeepMind unveiled Gemini Robotics 2, a suite of models that enable humanoid and other robots to coordinate full-body movements—walking, crouching, dexterous manipulation and multi-robot collaboration—while reasoning about tasks and changing environments. Unlike earlier versions focused mainly on arms and hands, the new system provides “whole-body intelligence.” One model is available to developers; the release marks a notable step toward more capable physical AI, though real-world deployment still faces safety and reliability hurdles.
