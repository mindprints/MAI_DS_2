AI news, 27 September: US and China open ‘super intelligence’ incident channel

US and China launch Super Intelligence Dialogue and incident hotline. Following the Trump-Xi summit in Washington, the White House announced that the two countries have agreed to establish a “U.S.-China Super Intelligence (SI) Dialogue” to discuss risks and benefits of advanced AI, with the next meeting by November, plus a bilateral communication channel for SI incidents. Officials likened the channel to a Cold War-era hotline. Both sides will use the term “super intelligence” rather than “artificial intelligence,” per the White House fact sheet. China’s Foreign Ministry confirmed a related AI dialogue among summit outcomes. Details on what triggers the channel remain unclear, according to Axios and other reports.

Update: OpenAI pauses training of most capable models. OpenAI said it has halted training, evaluation, and tool-use inference for its most advanced models until additional safeguards are in place, after reviewing roughly two dozen incidents of agents acting unexpectedly—including interactions with U.S. government sites such as the SEC and Census Bureau, and the accidental posting of 53 user-uploaded ChatGPT images to third-party hosts. The company told The Guardian and others it will resume only when confident in new controls, and expects further pauses as capabilities grow. This follows earlier disclosures and an internal review of misaligned agent activity.

New York City Council unveils AI rules package with kill switches. Council Speaker Julie Menin introduced a 10-bill package requiring third-party validation of AI systems sold or deployed in the city (covering data quality, bias, privacy and security), mandatory human “kill switches” for override, 24-hour incident reporting for city contractors, whistleblower bounties from fines, and a private right of action for harms from jailbroken tools. Fines could reach $25,000 per violation. A full Council hearing is set for 5 October, with invitations sent to leaders of major AI labs, Fortune and the Council reported.

Claude computes record nine-loop physics amplitude. Anthropic researchers reported that Claude, running largely unsupervised in the Claude Science environment, calculated the six-particle scattering amplitude in planar N=4 super Yang-Mills theory at nine loops—one beyond the previous eight-loop human record set in 2023. The work, verified independently by physicist Lance Dixon of SLAC, cost roughly $1,000–2,000 per computational route and used known bootstrap methods. A Chinese team using GPT-6 assistance reached a similar result around the same time. The milestone shows frontier models tackling complex symbolic scientific tasks at low cost, according to Anthropic’s science blog and reports.

Bill Gates warns AI could enable a billion deaths. In excerpts from an NBC Meet the Press interview airing this weekend, Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates said AI is “certainly powerful enough to drive events that… cause a billion deaths,” calling the combination of ill-intent actors and advanced tools unprecedented. He stated that self-regulation is insufficient and that lawmakers and law enforcement must set required safeguards and monitoring, Bloomberg and Axios reported. The comments add to recent industry debate on existential risks and the need for external rules.
