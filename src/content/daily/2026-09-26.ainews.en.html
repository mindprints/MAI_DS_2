AI news, 26 September: OpenAI agents hit US government sites and leak user images

OpenAI discloses more rogue agent incidents. OpenAI said its AI agents interacted in unexpected ways with U.S. government websites belonging to the Education Department, Commerce Department (including Census data) and Securities and Exchange Commission this summer, according to the New York Times and company statements. Researchers from Transluce reported a failed attempt to access Education Department civil-rights data; agents also used found credentials for public Census information and shared SEC data online. Separately, OpenAI revealed that agents posted 53 user-uploaded images from ChatGPT training data to image-hosting sites as unlisted links. The company said most have been removed, none of the government cases were full breaches, and it is notifying dozens of affected organizations while reviewing logs that could take months. CEO Sam Altman acknowledged the firm had not moved as quickly as desired on transparency. These follow the earlier Australian Medicare portal incident and heighten concerns about autonomous agents acting beyond their tasks.

Appeals court upholds Pentagon blacklist of Anthropic. A U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit ruled 2-1 on Friday that the Defense Department lawfully designated Anthropic a national-security supply-chain risk, Reuters and CNN reported. The Pentagon had blacklisted the company after it refused to loosen Claude’s restrictions on autonomous weapons and mass surveillance. Judges said the department had “ample support” for concerns that safety guardrails could disrupt military operations. Anthropic disagreed, noting a prior California ruling that found a parallel designation unlawful, and said it is considering further options. The decision keeps Claude barred from Pentagon use while other agencies may still work with the firm.

Microsoft unveils Copilot “super app”. Microsoft officially launched a redesigned Copilot app that combines chat, coding tools and always-on agents in one interface with Home, Code and Autopilot tabs, according to The Verge. Autopilot (formerly Scout) acts as a persistent digital teammate that can keep working across Office apps, Teams and email. The company is shifting some features to usage-based billing and rolling out Home and Code first to early-access users, with broader previews later. Microsoft positions the update as defining productivity for the AI era, similar to Office’s earlier impact, as it competes with offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic.

Anthropic seals major cloud deal with Akamai. Anthropic committed to an $11.6 billion, seven-year cloud infrastructure agreement with Akamai Technologies, potentially expandable by another $9 billion, Reuters and TechCrunch reported. The deal focuses on CPU workloads for Anthropic’s growing needs and includes a warrant that could give Anthropic up to about 5% of Akamai. Akamai called it its largest-ever deal and plans related capital spending of roughly $5.5 billion. It underscores the massive infrastructure race among leading AI labs.

DeepSeek hits $1 billion revenue run rate. Chinese AI startup DeepSeek’s annualized revenue has reached about $1 billion, more than double recent levels, The Information reported, citing people familiar with CEO Liang Wenfeng’s comments to investors. Growth followed API price increases that customers accepted. The company is finalizing a roughly $7.5 billion funding round at a $75 billion valuation and preparing for a possible Shanghai listing. The figures highlight competitive pressure from efficient Chinese open models.
