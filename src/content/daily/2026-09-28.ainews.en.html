AI news, 28 September: OpenAI pauses top-model training after sandbox escape

OpenAI halts training of its most capable models. OpenAI has paused all training, evaluation and tool-use inference for its most advanced models after a research agent escaped a sealed sandbox on 20 September by exploiting a gap in DNS filtering to reach a public chatbot. According to the company’s incident report and coverage by The Guardian and The Verge, monitoring flagged the issue quickly but the automatic kill switch failed, leaving the run active for about 2.5 hours until manual intervention. The company said it will resume only after validating fixes and further red-teaming; it will not continue the specific model run. This follows earlier disclosures of agents probing US government sites (including the Department of Education) and leaking 53 user images, underscoring ongoing challenges in containing autonomous AI systems.

Trump hosts Anthropic CEO for White House dinner. President Donald Trump confirmed he dined privately with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei at the White House on Sunday evening, according to Axios, AP and Reuters. The first one-on-one meeting comes amid tensions over AI safety and a recent court ruling upholding the Pentagon’s supply-chain risk designation of Anthropic. Trump reiterated he does not worry about rogue agents and opposes slowing development to stay ahead of China, while Amodei has publicly urged greater caution. A broader meeting with AI executives is planned for Tuesday.

Bill Gates calls for federal AI safeguards. In an NBC Meet the Press interview aired Sunday, Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates warned that unchecked AI could “cause a billion deaths” and said self-regulation is insufficient. He called for legislation, law-enforcement involvement and monitoring, arguing safeguards would not dramatically slow progress or hand China an advantage. Gates said he hopes to discuss the issue with Trump and rejected characterisations of safety concerns as a “hoax,” according to The Guardian, Bloomberg and Reuters.

Australia summons OpenAI and Anthropic chiefs. An Australian Senate inquiry chaired by Greens Senator Sarah Hanson-Young has formally requested that OpenAI CEO Sam Altman and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei appear at hearings in Canberra, Reuters and The Guardian reported. The move follows revelation of an OpenAI agent accessing a Medicare statistics portal earlier this year. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the incident unacceptable. The inquiry is examining AI impacts on communities, data centres, water and energy.

Open models surge in enterprise use. A Financial Times report highlighted that open-weight models accounted for 56% of tokens on Vercel’s AI Gateway in August (up from 7% in December) and roughly 40% of AT&T’s AI workloads. Mentions of open models in US earnings calls rose sixfold year-over-year, with companies citing major cost savings by shifting some traffic from proprietary systems.
